# Australian CPI public guide

A plain-English introduction to Australia's Consumer Price Index (CPI), with a guide to all **87 expenditure classes** in the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) classification. It is for readers who want to understand what sits behind an inflation release and why a price observed online may tell only part of the story.

## What is here

- **[CPI_CLASS_GUIDE.md](CPI_CLASS_GUIDE.md)** describes the classes, organised into the 11 CPI groups.
- **This README** explains the main concepts and points to official sources.

This is a two-document educational reference, not a forecasting service. It contains no forecasts, model specifications, performance results, executable code or underlying price datasets. It is not an ABS publication or an investment recommendation.

## What the CPI measures

The CPI tracks changes in the prices of goods and services acquired by households. Australia's headline measure combines the eight capital-city indexes. It represents a broad spending basket, not the exact expenses of a particular person or family. A household that spends more on rent, transport or health can experience different price pressures from the headline figure. See the [ABS CPI frequently asked questions](https://www.abs.gov.au/articles/frequently-asked-questions-faqs-about-consumer-price-index).

An **expenditure class** is a category of related purchases, such as milk, rents or dental services. Classes sit inside broader groups. They do not receive equal importance: expenditure weights reflect the relative amounts households spend. A large price move in a small class need not have a large effect on overall inflation. The [ABS coverage and classification chapter](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/detailed-methodology-information/concepts-sources-methods/consumer-price-index-concepts-sources-and-methods/2025/coverage-and-classifications) explains this structure.

## Reading an inflation release

- **Index level versus inflation rate:** an index measures relative prices; an inflation rate describes its change over a stated period. An index is not a dollar price.
- **Monthly versus annual change:** these compare different intervals. Always check the reference period before comparing figures.
- **Price versus spending:** a larger bill can reflect buying more, choosing a different product or paying a higher price. These are not interchangeable.
- **Original versus seasonally adjusted data:** seasonal adjustment aims to separate recurring calendar patterns from other movements. Adjusted series can be revised.
- **Headline versus underlying inflation:** headline CPI covers the full basket. Measures such as the trimmed mean reduce the influence of unusually large movements; they answer a related but different question.

The ABS introduced the complete monthly CPI for the October 2025 reference month and continues selected quarterly series. Older references to the monthly CPI *indicator* describe a different publication. Use the [ABS concepts, sources and methods](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/detailed-methodology-information/concepts-sources-methods/consumer-price-index-concepts-sources-and-methods/2025) and the release's own explanatory notes when comparing periods.

## Why an online price is not the CPI

A web page can show an advertised price without showing what households actually paid, how much they bought, or whether a discount was widely available. Pack sizes, product quality, service inclusions, location and eligibility for rebates can also differ. Travel prices depend on the trip as well as the booking date; an advertised rent is not the rent paid by every existing tenant.

Public price observations can help explain a category, but one retailer, tariff or advertised offer should not be treated as the official index. The [ABS price collection chapter](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/detailed-methodology-information/concepts-sources-methods/consumer-price-index-concepts-sources-and-methods/2025/price-collection) describes how the official measure handles these issues. This repository does not provide a collection or scraping procedure.
